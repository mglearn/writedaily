# Daily Writing a Day: Fix It + Write, 2026–27

A Daily Oral Language (DOL) sentence and a TEKS-aligned writing prompt for every school day, grades K–2, 3–5, and 6–8.
Codes refer to the Texas ELAR TEKS (19 TAC §110.2–110.24): K–5 writing process (11), genres (12), inquiry (13); 6–8 writing process (10), genres (11), inquiry (12). Sub-letters can vary slightly by grade.

## Week 1 (2026-08-17): New Beginnings · First Days, Fresh Starts

Students launch the year by telling true stories about new beginnings, building a daily writing habit and the check that every sentence is complete. *Note: first week of school.*

| Band | Genre | Conventions focus | I can |
|---|---|---|---|
| K–2 | Personal narrative (12)(A) | Capitalize the first word of a sentence | I can tell about something that happened to me and start each sentence with a capital letter. |
| 3–5 | Personal narrative (12)(A) | Complete sentences, not fragments | I can write a true story with a clear beginning, and every sentence is complete. |
| 6–8 | Personal narrative (11)(A) | Sentence fragments | I can write a personal narrative that shows feelings through details and has no sentence fragments. |

### Monday: Plan · Picking a First to Tell

**K–2**
- Fix It (2 errors): my new backpack is blue
- Fixed: My new backpack is blue.
- Write (11)(A): Draw three small pictures of your first days of school this year: something new, something fun, and something a little scary. Circle the one you want to tell about.

**3–5**
- Fix It (3 errors): On the first day, Lucia found her new classroom. Next to the libary.
- Fixed: On the first day, Lucia found her new classroom next to the library.
- Write (11)(A): List five “firsts” from your life: first lost tooth, first time on a bike, first day somewhere new. Star the one you remember best and jot three details: where you were, who was there, and how you felt.

**6–8**
- Fix It (3 errors): Before the first bell rang, Andre checked his schedule twice. Because he didnt want to get lost in the new building.
- Fixed: Before the first bell rang, Andre checked his schedule twice because he didn't want to get lost in the new building.
- Write (10)(A): List times you started something new: a school, a team, a neighborhood, a friend group, a job at home. Pick one and sketch a quick timeline: the beginning, the moment it got real, and how you felt after.

*Teacher:* Model your own list or sketch first, out loud. Before time ends, every student should have one idea circled or starred.

### Tuesday: Draft · Starting the Story

**K–2**
- Fix It (3 errors): we walkd to our new room
- Fixed: We walked to our new room.
- Write (11)(B): Use the picture you circled yesterday, or think about this morning at school. Write: “On the first day, I…” Then draw what you saw.

**3–5**
- Fix It (3 errors): Mrs. Ortiz smiled at the class. And wrote her name on the bord.
- Fixed: Mrs. Ortiz smiled at the class and wrote her name on the board.
- Write (11)(B): Use your starred idea from Monday, or pick any first you remember well. Write the beginning of the story: put us right there, with where you were and the first thing you saw or heard.

**6–8**
- Fix It (3 errors): walking into the cafeteria with a full tray. Andre searched for a familar face.
- Fixed: Walking into the cafeteria with a full tray, Andre searched for a familiar face.
- Write (10)(B): Draft the opening paragraph of a story about a new beginning (Monday's timeline or a fresh one). Start in the middle of the action, not with “One day.” Stop right before the big moment.

*Teacher:* Point out that the Fix It sentence starts with a capital. Look for story beginnings that name a real place and a real moment.

### Wednesday: Revise · Show the Feeling

**K–2**
- Fix It (3 errors): my frend sat by me
- Fixed: My friend sat by me.
- Write (11)(C): Listen: “I went to school.” Now add a feeling: “I walked into school with butterflies in my tummy.” Add one feeling word to your own story.

**3–5**
- Fix It (3 errors): During recess, we played tag. Until the whistle blew
- Fixed: During recess, we played tag until the whistle blew.
- Write (11)(C): Read this: “I went in the room. It was nice. I sat down.” Craft move: **show, don't tell**. Swap “nice” for what you saw, heard, or smelled (new crayons? a humming fan?). Find one telling word in your story and replace it with a sensory detail.

**6–8**
- Fix It (3 errors): The new schedule confused everyone. Especially the sixth graders who kept walking into the wrong classroom
- Fixed: The new schedule confused everyone, especially the sixth graders who kept walking into the wrong classroom.
- Write (10)(C): Weak model: “I was nervous on my first day. It was really scary. I was so nervous.” Craft move: **show the feeling through the body and actions**: sweaty palms, rereading the same text, a locker that won't open. Rewrite two sentences of your draft so readers feel it without you naming it.

*Teacher:* Read the weak model aloud, then revise it together before students touch their own work. Look for one real change, not a recopied draft.

### Thursday: Quick-write · First Bite, First Try

**K–2**
- Fix It (2 errors): can I sit here
- Fixed: Can I sit here?
- Write (12)(A): Tell about a time you tried a new food. What was it? Did you like it? Draw your face when you tasted it!

**3–5**
- Fix It (3 errors): my little brother started kindergarten. With a dinosaur lunchbox.
- Fixed: My little brother started kindergarten with a dinosaur lunchbox.
- Write (12)(A): Quick-write: tell the true story of a time you tried something that scared you a little: a high dive, a new food, reading aloud, a sleepover. Write for six minutes without stopping to erase.

**6–8**
- Fix It (4 errors): Since the bus was late on the first morning. Half the class missed homeroom, and the principel made an announcment.
- Fixed: Since the bus was late on the first morning, half the class missed homeroom, and the principal made an announcement.
- Write (11)(A): Fast write: the first time you did something on your own, like cooking a meal, walking to a store, or watching a younger kid. Tell it as a story in seven minutes and include one line of what you were thinking.

*Teacher:* Set a timer and write alongside students. The goal is fluency; spelling waits until Friday.

### Friday: Edit & share · Check Every Sentence

**K–2**
- Fix It (3 errors): school is fun. we like our class
- Fixed: School is fun. We like our class.
- Write (11)(D), (11)(E): Pick your best sentence from this week. Does every sentence start with a **capital letter**? Fix any that don't. Then read it to a partner and show your picture.

**3–5**
- Fix It (3 errors): We have a new class pet. A turtle named Speedy. he lives by the window.
- Fixed: We have a new class pet, a turtle named Speedy. He lives by the window.
- Write (11)(D), (11)(E): Choose one story from this week and edit for **complete sentences**: read each one aloud and ask, “Who or what? Doing what?” Join any fragment to the sentence next to it. Then read your best two sentences in the author's chair.

**6–8**
- Fix It (3 errors): Andre survived his first week. Even though he got lost twice. By Friday, he knew every shortcut in the building
- Fixed: Andre survived his first week even though he got lost twice. By Friday, he knew every shortcut in the building.
- Write (10)(D), (10)(E): Edit one piece from this week for **sentence fragments**: read it from the last sentence to the first so each one has to stand alone. Fix any “because…” or “-ing” fragments. Then share with a partner, who names the line that felt most real.

*Teacher:* Model the check on one student's sentence (with permission) before partners start. Celebrate fixes, not perfection. (3–5 Fix It: also accept a colon or dash before "a turtle"; 6–8: also accept a comma before "even though.")

## Week 2 (2026-08-24): Friends and Teamwork · Better Together

Students write true stories about friends and teams, zooming in on the moment a group came together or fell apart, while they practice ending sentences correctly.

| Band | Genre | Conventions focus | I can |
|---|---|---|---|
| K–2 | Personal narrative (12)(A) | End marks: period and question mark | I can tell a story about a friend and end each sentence with a period or a question mark. |
| 3–5 | Personal narrative (12)(A) | Capital letters and end punctuation | I can write a story about teamwork with dialogue, and I use capital letters and end punctuation correctly. |
| 6–8 | Personal narrative (11)(A) | Run-ons and comma splices | I can write a narrative that zooms in on a turning point, with no run-ons or comma splices. |

### Monday: Plan · Mapping Teamwork Moments

**K–2**
- Fix It (2 errors): do you want to play
- Fixed: Do you want to play?
- Write (11)(A): Think of a time you played or worked with a friend. Draw it. Tell a partner: Who was there? What did you do together?

**3–5**
- Fix It (3 errors): rosa and kenji built a fort out of couch cushions
- Fixed: Rosa and Kenji built a fort out of couch cushions.
- Write (11)(A): Make a web with “Friends and Teamwork” in the middle. Add times you worked with someone: a game, a chore, a project, a fort. Pick the one with the most action and list three things that happened, in order.

**6–8**
- Fix It (3 errors): Our science team had one hour to build a bridge out of straws, nobody agreed on the desing
- Fixed: Our science team had one hour to build a bridge out of straws, but nobody agreed on the design.
- Write (10)(A): Brainstorm three moments when a group either clicked or fell apart: a team, a group project, a band, a group chat. Choose one and jot the problem, the turning point, and what it taught you about working with people.

*Teacher:* Share a short teamwork story of your own as a model. Look for a chosen moment, not just a topic like “soccer.” (6–8 Fix It: also accept a period or semicolon instead of the comma + conjunction.)

### Tuesday: Draft · Drafting the Middle

**K–2**
- Fix It (3 errors): we bilt a big tower
- Fixed: We built a big tower.
- Write (11)(B): Write about a time you and a friend did something together. Start with “One day, my friend and I…” End with how you felt.

**3–5**
- Fix It (4 errors): kenji held the blanket while Rosa tied the knots did it stay up
- Fixed: Kenji held the blanket while Rosa tied the knots. Did it stay up?
- Write (11)(B): Use your web from Monday, or think of a time a team helped you. Draft the middle of the story: what went wrong, and what did you and your teammate say and do to fix it? Include one line of talking.

**6–8**
- Fix It (3 errors): Maya wanted to paint the bridge red, Jordan wanted to leave it plain, they argued until the bell rang.
- Fixed: Maya wanted to paint the bridge red, but Jordan wanted to leave it plain. They argued until the bell rang.
- Write (10)(B): Draft the turning point of your teamwork story (Monday's idea or a new one): the moment the group came together or broke apart. Slow it down: what was said, who looked at whom, what changed.

*Teacher:* Remind students that the middle is where the problem lives. Look for at least one line where someone speaks. (6–8 Fix It: also accept a period or semicolon instead of the comma + conjunction.)

### Wednesday: Revise · Let Them Talk

**K–2**
- Fix It (2 errors): will you play with me
- Fixed: Will you play with me?
- Write (11)(C): Listen: “We played.” That is short! Add what and where: “We played tag by the big slide.” Find one short sentence in your story and add where it happened.

**3–5**
- Fix It (4 errors): Our team won the game on saturday we cheered so loudly
- Fixed: Our team won the game on Saturday. We cheered so loudly.
- Write (11)(C): Weak model: “We played. We won. It was fun.” Craft move: **add dialogue**. Let the people talk: “Pass it to me!” Kenji yelled. Add one line of dialogue to your own teamwork story.

**6–8**
- Fix It (3 errors): We finally tested the bridge, it held twelve textbooks. Which was a class record.
- Fixed: We finally tested the bridge, and it held twelve textbooks, which was a class record.
- Write (10)(C): Weak model: “The group project was hard, but we worked it out in the end.” Craft move: **zoom in on one small moment**: an eye roll, a muttered comment, someone finally laughing. Replace one summary sentence in your draft with a three- or four-sentence zoomed-in scene.

*Teacher:* Revise the weak model together on the board first. Look for students adding words to their draft, not starting over. (6–8 Fix It: also accept a period or semicolon instead of the comma + conjunction.)

### Thursday: Quick-write · Learning Someone's Game

**K–2**
- Fix It (2 errors): who is your best friend
- Fixed: Who is your best friend?
- Write (12)(A): Tell about a time someone taught you a game, or you taught someone else. What happened when you played? Draw and write.

**3–5**
- Fix It (4 errors): have you ever played on a team with your cousin my cousin Ana is a great goalie
- Fixed: Have you ever played on a team with your cousin? My cousin Ana is a great goalie.
- Write (12)(A): Quick-write: tell about a time you and a sibling, cousin, neighbor, or friend had to share one thing: the last slice, the front seat, one controller. How did it turn out?

**6–8**
- Fix It (3 errors): My friends and I tried to make pancakes for the whole team, the first batch burned the smoke alarm went off.
- Fixed: My friends and I tried to make pancakes for the whole team, but the first batch burned, and the smoke alarm went off.
- Write (11)(A): Fast write: a time you were the new person in a group that already knew each other, like a team, a club, or a lunch table. Tell the moment you went from outsider to part of it (or didn't).

*Teacher:* Write fast with the class. Invite two volunteers to read one sentence aloud. (6–8 Fix It: also accept a period or semicolon instead of the comma + conjunction.)

### Friday: Edit & share · End Marks Matter

**K–2**
- Fix It (3 errors): is it time to play. Yes, it is
- Fixed: Is it time to play? Yes, it is.
- Write (11)(D), (11)(E): Look at your story from this week. Does each telling sentence end with a **period**? Does each asking sentence end with a **question mark**? Fix them, then read your story to a partner.

**3–5**
- Fix It (3 errors): our class team is called the Comets. who thinks we can win
- Fixed: Our class team is called the Comets. Who thinks we can win?
- Write (11)(D), (11)(E): Edit one story from this week for **capital letters and end punctuation**: a capital at every sentence start and for names; a period, question mark, or exclamation point at every end. Then share with a partner, who asks you one question about your story.

**6–8**
- Fix It (3 errors): The team lost the final game, nobody was angry. Coach Ruiz bought everyone tacos, we laughed about the missed goal
- Fixed: The team lost the final game, but nobody was angry. Coach Ruiz bought everyone tacos, and we laughed about the missed goal.
- Write (10)(D), (10)(E): Edit one piece for **run-ons and comma splices**: find every comma that joins two complete thoughts and fix it with a period, a semicolon, or a comma plus *and*, *but*, or *so*. Share your turning-point scene in the author's chair.

*Teacher:* Have students point to each end mark as they reread. Listen during sharing for voices that drop at periods and rise at questions. (6–8 Fix It: also accept a period or semicolon instead of the comma + conjunction.)

## Week 4 (2026-09-07): Helpers and Jobs · How Helpers Do It

| Band | Genre | Conventions focus | I can |

- Fix It (2 errors): are you ready for summer
- Fixed: Are you ready for summer?
- Write (12)(A): Write about a time this year when you made someone laugh, or someone made you laugh.

**3–5**
- Fix It (3 errors): Next year Ill join the choir and my friends will to.
- Fixed: Next year I'll join the choir, and my friends will too.
- Write (12)(A): Quick-write: Tell the story of the funniest or strangest thing that happened in our classroom this year, as if you were telling it to next year's class.

**6–8**
- Fix It (3 errors): next year I have three goals, joining the robotics club, reading more books, and speak up in class.
- Fixed: Next year I have three goals: joining the robotics club, reading more books, and speaking up in class.
- Write (11)(A): Quick-write: Write the story of a moment from this year that you'll probably still be telling people about in ten years. Why that one?

*Teacher:* Keep it joyful and quick. Remind students to keep classmates' feelings in mind in funny stories. In the 6–8 DOL, also accept a dash in place of the colon.

### Friday: Edit & share · Last Author's Chair

**K–2**
- Fix It (3 errors): i cant wait for next year. It will be fun
- Fixed: I can't wait for next year. It will be fun.
- Write (11)(D), (11)(E): Pick your best story from this week. Check capitals, end marks, and spelling. Fix two things. Then share it in the author's chair one last time this year!

**3–5**
- Fix It (3 errors): this year I learned to write dialogue. "i can do it" I told myself.
- Fixed: This year I learned to write dialogue. "I can do it," I told myself.
- Write (11)(D), (11)(E): Edit your best story from the week for the whole year's conventions: capitals, end marks, commas, apostrophes, and spelling. Then share in a final author's chair, or read your favorite sentence in a class whip-around.

**6–8**
- Fix It (3 errors): This year taught me a lot. Its strange how much a persons outlook can change in nine months
- Fixed: This year taught me a lot. It's strange how much a person's outlook can change in nine months.
- Write (10)(D), (10)(E): Edit your strongest piece from the week using everything you have learned this year: fragments and run-ons, commas, apostrophes, usage, and pronoun clarity. Then share a paragraph in a final read-around; each listener gives you one word that describes your writing.

*Teacher:* Edit, then celebrate. Every student who wants to share gets a turn; consider ending with applause for a year of writing.
