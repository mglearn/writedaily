ELPS Supports

How to use the daily Fix It and Write routine with emergent bilingual (EB) students, aligned to the Texas English Language Proficiency Standards (19 TAC §74.4).

Start from assets. Students bring a home language, cultural knowledge, and a lifetime of stories. Fix It teaches the conventions of standard written English for school and public audiences. It is never a correction of how a family speaks.

The routine is already a language routine

Every day students listen to a sentence read aloud, speak to explain a fix, read a prompt, and write. That covers all four ELPS domains in ten minutes. The ELPS cross-curricular student expectations below are paraphrased and keyed to their numbers in §74.4(c).

Cross-language notes for the Fix It focus

Some conventions work differently in other languages. Naming the difference helps students transfer what they know instead of treating it as a mistake.

  • Capitalizing days, months, and languages (weeks 13, 22): Spanish, French, and Portuguese write lunes, octubre, español in lowercase. English capitalizes them.
  • Plural nouns (weeks 5, 21): Vietnamese, Chinese, and Korean usually don't mark plurals on the noun. Expect "two dog" and teach the -s as the signal English uses.
  • Past tense (weeks 9, 12): Vietnamese and Chinese show time with words like yesterday, not verb endings. Point to the time word in the sentence, then to the -ed.
  • Sentence fragments (week 1): Spanish can drop the subject (Fue al parque). English needs it: He went to the park.
  • Adjectives (weeks 6, 23): Spanish often puts the adjective after the noun (perro grande); English puts it before (big dog).
  • Question and exclamation marks (weeks 2, 10, 31): Spanish opens with ¿ and ¡. English uses only the closing mark.
  • Cognates help with spelling and vocabulary in Spanish: familia/family, animal/animal, información/information. Watch for false friends such as embarazada.

Five quick wins

  • Read everything aloud. The sentence, the prompt, and the fix. Listening comes before writing.
  • Post a word bank for the week's theme on Monday and add to it all week.
  • Pair thoughtfully. A partner who shares the home language can help with Monday's plan; an English-proficient partner can help with Friday's edit.
  • Let planning happen in any language. Ideas first, English second.
  • Use the band toggle. The K–2 version of the same day is a ready-made scaffold for older newcomers.
